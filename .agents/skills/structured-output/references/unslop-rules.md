# Unslop rules

Apply each rule to every sentence in the reply. Rule numbers match the unslop skill. A missing number is an unused id. Leave that number unused.

After the numbered rules, ask what still makes the reply read as generated. Delete that sentence, or replace it with a fact, a decision, or a next action.

Write the reply in whole sentences. Rule 33's short-line exception does not apply to it.

## Word choice

Pick every word with the simple-words skill and its list at [../../simple-words/references/word-swaps.md](../../simple-words/references/word-swaps.md). That list covers AI vocabulary, fancy substitutes for "is", filler phrases, abstract stand-in nouns and plain words. Use one name for one thing, and repeat it.

## Content

3. **Trailing -ing phrases.** Delete a phrase such as "highlighting", "ensuring", "reflecting", "showcasing", or "fostering" when it adds no source. If a real source is named, keep the source and cut the -ing frame.

5. **Vague attributions.** Delete "Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", and "Some critics argue", or replace the phrase with the named source.

## Language

9. **Paired contrast.** When a sentence says "not just X, but Y", state Y and delete the contrast.

10. **Forced groups of three.** Use the number of items the facts support. Do not pad or cut a list to three.

12. **False ranges.** When "from X to Y" does not sit on a real scale, list the topics instead.

## Style

13. **Dashes and asides.** Use periods or commas. Do not use em dashes, en dashes, parentheses, or a hyphen standing in for a dash.

14. **Colons as connectors.** A colon may introduce a list or an example. Do not use a colon to join two clauses.

15. **Boldface.** Bold the section labels only. The thread title is a heading. Do not bold ordinary nouns.

16. **Labels that repeat the line.** Rewrite a bold label and colon that restates the same line. A section label on its own line, with the detail in the next paragraph, may stay.

17. **Heading case.** Use sentence case for headings.

19. **Curly quotes.** Replace curly quotes with straight quotes.

## Communication artifacts

20. **Chatbot phrases.** Delete phrases such as "I hope this helps", "Let me know if", "Of course", "Certainly", and "Found the smoking gun".

22. **Praise before the point.** Delete "Great question" and "You're absolutely right". State the point.

## Filler

24. **Stacked hedges.** A stack such as "could potentially possibly" becomes "may", or it becomes a direct statement when the fact is known.

25. **Empty closings.** Delete a closing that states no plan and no fact. Summary must state what was done.

## Plain speech

27. **Feeling instead of mechanism.** If a sentence names a feeling, or could be pasted unchanged into another project's docs, replace it with the mechanism, the instruction, or the number. If you cannot, delete it.

28. **Dense sentences.** If the reader must reread a sentence, split it or drop a clause. One idea per sentence.

29. **Active voice.** Name the actor. "The compiler validates queries" stays. "Queries are validated" becomes that sentence when the actor is known. Passive voice stays only when the actor is unknown.

30. **Adverbs on a weak verb.** Cut the adverb, or use a stronger verb. When you have a measurement, write the number.

32. **Mannered prose.** Replace a metaphor, an aphorism, or a personified object with the literal phrase. Word choice covers the abstract nouns.

33. **Dropped words.** Restore the articles and verbs. Spell out an arrow or an abbreviation the reader would otherwise have to decode. In the reply, do this even when another skill asks for fragments.
